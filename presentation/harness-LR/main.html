Thesis · 02

Practitioners know harnesses matter.
Research lacks the vocabulary.

The infrastructure layer around an LLM is folklore in production: known to work, hard to name. OpenAI credits harness engineering for producing a million lines of code without manual writing. Anthropic builds simple, inspectable agents and tools designed for agents, not humans. Martin Fowler frames the harness as a cybernetic governor for AI agents.

The research literature studies memory in isolation, tools in isolation, planning in isolation, safety in isolation. What is missing is the system that integrates these into reliable operation — and a shared vocabulary to talk about it.

This survey is that vocabulary.

AOpenAI — “harness engineering” coined as a discipline name in 2026.

BAnthropic — emphasis on inspectability and on tools sized to agent perception, not human ergonomics.

CFowler — the harness as a cybernetic governor; a control system, not a wrapper.

DToday researchers and practitioners are looking at the same artifacts under different names.

Part Three — Cross-Layer Synthesis

Three forces, not isolated layers.

Cost–Quality–Speed Trilemma. Stronger checks slow execution and inflate cost. Pick two; the third moves. Harness budgets are budget tradeoffs, written in latency and dollars.

Capability–Control Tradeoff. More authority widens the blast radius. Power without governance is liability; governance without power is a demo.

Harness Coupling. Local changes regress the whole control loop. Test harness changes as system changes — never as isolated tweaks.

iThe ecosystem is shifting from build an agent to operate a fleet of agents.

iiFrameworks are becoming platforms — tenancy, billing, fault recovery, multi-region runtime.

iiiScore variance cannot be attributed to the model alone; the harness owns half the variance.

Cross-Layer Synthesis
Once the seven layers are composed, their interactions create constraints no single layer can solve. Harness changes should be tested as system changes; the ecosystem is shifting from "build an agent" to "operate a fleet of agents."

Open Problems · Five questions for the next phase

What stays hard after the taxonomy.

  1. i

    Harden execution under real threat models

    Scale sandboxes from research prototypes to production targets that adversaries will actively probe.

  2. ii

    Maintain reliable state across long horizons

    Multi-session, multi-week work demands more than larger context windows.

  3. iii

    Diagnose failures from traces, not scores

    Final outcomes hide the failure modes that traces expose. Build for the trace, not the leaderboard.

  4. iv

    Standardize handoffs across agents, tools, sandboxes, humans

    Composition needs a wire format and a contract, not a custom integration each time.

  5. v

    Keep harnesses useful as models improve — simplify, do not bloat

    Anthropic removed scaffolding upgrading Opus 4.5 → 4.6; cost fell from $200 to $125 with equal quality. Every harness component encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do — those assumptions go stale.

Open Problems
Every harness component encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do on its own. Those assumptions go stale as models improve. Anthropic's Opus 4.5 → 4.6 upgrade let them strip scaffolding and cut cost from $200 to $125 with equal quality.
“Every harness component encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do on its own.

Anthropic · Harness Design for Long-Running Apps · 2026

Key Quote
The paper's single most memorable line. Harness components encode model-limitation assumptions; when models improve, the assumptions go stale and the scaffolding should come off. Removing context resets cut Anthropic's cost 37% with no quality drop.

Takeaways · What to do on Monday

Treat the harness as an independent engineering surface.

  1. 01

    Infrastructure quality, not model size, sets the reliability ceiling

    The binding constraint is the harness. Budget the work accordingly.

  2. 02

    Use ETCLOVG to partition and improve harness layers

    Seven layers; one shared vocabulary across teams and across papers.

  3. 03

    Observability and Governance deserve dedicated ownership

    They are not appendices; they are first-class. Staff them that way.

  4. 04

    Evaluate the model–harness pair; the trace is the primary evidence

    A leaderboard score is an aggregate of pair behavior, not a property of the model.

  5. 05

    Simplify as models improve; do not assume more scaffolding is better

    Adaptive simplification is the durable goal. Re-estimate every harness assumption each upgrade.
